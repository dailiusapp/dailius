# Dailius Design System & Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** July 31, 2026

---

# Brand Mission

Dailius helps people make better use of their limited time.

It is not another calendar.

It is an AI life planner that intelligently fits goals, hobbies, work, family, fitness, and personal projects around existing commitments.

The product should always feel calm, trustworthy, and helpful.

---

# Brand Personality

Dailius should feel:

* Calm
* Intelligent
* Helpful
* Trustworthy
* Intentional
* Optimistic
* Modern
* Minimal

Avoid feeling:

* Busy
* Flashy
* Overly futuristic
* Gamified
* Corporate
* Cold
* Robotic

---

# Brand Voice

The UI should communicate like a thoughtful assistant.

Use clear, concise language.

Prefer:

* "Let's find a better time."
* "Here's a better plan."
* "Your schedule has been updated."

Avoid:

* Buzzwords
* AI hype
* Marketing clichés
* Technical jargon

The assistant should explain decisions simply and confidently.

---

# Tagline

Primary:

> **Plan your life, not just your calendar.**

---

# Logo

The primary logo consists of:

* A rounded capital **D**
* The D incorporates a subtle clock
* The clock represents intelligent time planning
* The clock should never overpower the D
* The logo should remain recognizable at favicon size

Wordmark:

**Dailius**

* Dark navy lettering
* The letters **"ai"** inside "Dailius" use the same blue gradient as the favicon
* This subtly reinforces that Dailius is AI-powered without being distracting

Do not:

* Stretch
* Rotate
* Add shadows
* Add glow effects
* Change colors
* Separate the "ai" from the word

---

# Favicon

The favicon is the D-shaped clock icon only.

Characteristics:

* Thick rounded strokes
* Flat vector style
* Blue → indigo gradient
* Simple clock hands
* No glow
* No drop shadow
* No border

Must remain recognizable at:

* 16×16
* 32×32
* 48×48

# Layout Philosophy

Every screen should feel:

* Spacious
* Calm
* Easy to scan

Prefer whitespace over dense layouts.

Use an 8-point spacing system.

Never cram information onto the screen.

---

# Cards

Cards should have:

* White background
* Rounded corners
* Thin border
* Very subtle shadow
* Generous padding

Cards should feel lightweight.

---

# Buttons

## Primary

* Blue gradient
* White text
* Rounded corners
* Medium weight

## Secondary

* White background
* Navy border
* Navy text

Buttons should never use excessive shadows or animations.

---

# Icons

Use simple outline icons.

Icons should be:

* Clean
* Minimal
* Easy to recognize

Avoid:

* 3D icons
* Cartoon illustrations
* Skeuomorphic graphics

---

# Illustrations

Illustrations should communicate:

* Planning
* Time
* Balance
* Organization
* Focus

Avoid robots, brains, glowing AI imagery, circuits, or science-fiction themes.

---

# Motion

Animations should be subtle.

Prefer:

* Fade
* Scale
* Gentle slide

Avoid:

* Bounce
* Spin
* Flash
* Dramatic motion

Animations should support usability, not attract attention.

---

# User Experience Principles

Every design decision should support these principles.

## 1. Reduce cognitive load

The interface should simplify planning, never complicate it.

## 2. AI should explain

Users should understand why recommendations were made.

## 3. Calm over urgency

Avoid making users feel behind or guilty.

## 4. Simplicity wins

If two solutions work equally well, choose the simpler one.

## 5. Consistency beats novelty

Reuse familiar components and interaction patterns.

## 6. Whitespace is a feature

Do not fill every empty space.

## 7. Planning should feel enjoyable

The experience should leave users feeling more in control, not overwhelmed.

---

# Visual Inspiration

The visual quality should feel closer to:

* Linear
* Stripe
* Notion
* Apple
* Arc Browser

Avoid looking like:

* Generic AI chatbots
* Crypto websites
* Gaming interfaces
* Neon cyberpunk designs

---

# Accessibility

Design for accessibility by default.

* Maintain strong color contrast
* Use clear typography
* Support keyboard navigation
* Avoid relying on color alone
* Provide meaningful labels for interactive elements

---

# Design Rule

When uncertain between two design choices, choose the one that is:

* Simpler
* Cleaner
* Easier to understand
* More timeless

The goal is to build software that people trust every day—not software that merely looks impressive.
